What these mean

Exposure Category
How rough or open the terrain is around the building, which affects how hard the wind hits it.
A Exposure A is the old “heavy urban/city-center” category, which is rarely used today.
B is urban/suburban or wooded terrain with lots of obstructions.
C is open terrain with scattered obstructions under 30 ft (the most common choice).
D is flat, unobstructed terrain facing open water. Use the category called out on the project's structural/civil drawings or geotechnical report.
Importance Factor
A multiplier for how critical the building is if it fails. Standard buildings use 1.0. Essential facilities (hospitals, fire/police stations, shelters) or buildings with high occupancy use a higher factor, typically 1.15. Confirm the correct value against the project's structural drawings before using it for design.
Pnet30
The reference net wind pressure for a small component area, straight from the code's exposure/wind-speed tables, before it's adjusted for this building's actual roof height or importance factor. It's an intermediate number used to get to Pnet, not the final design value.
Pnet
The final net design wind pressure (Pnet30 × Height Factor × Importance Factor), in pounds per square foot. This is the number to use for component and cladding design. A negative value means suction (pulling outward); a positive value means the wind is pushing inward.
Center vs. Corner
Center applies to the typical field of the wall or roof, away from edges. Corner applies to the higher-pressure zones near building corners and edges, where wind pressures are always greater — use Corner values for fasteners and components in those zones.
